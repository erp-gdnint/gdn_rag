GDAC grantee obligations and expectations: reference for the GDAC Coach

Version 0.2, November 2026. Drawn from the Project Operations Manual (March 2026), the standard grant agreement templates and the FUS submission guidelines

About this document

This document summarises what GDAC grantees are expected to do under their grant, across the three tracks: the Japanese Award for Outstanding Research on Development (ORD), the Japanese Award for Most Innovative Development Project (MIDP) and the Japan Social Development Fund Award (JSDF), together with the Connector Grant (CG). It is written for grantees and for the GDAC Coach.

It is a summary, not a legal document. Each grantee’s signed grant agreement, including its Annexes, is what applies to them. Where this document and a grantee’s own grant agreement differ, the grant agreement prevails. Dates, amounts and percentages vary from one grant to another; the figures below are typical examples taken from 2025 agreements and must never be read as the figures of a specific grant.

For any question about a specific grant, the grantee should contact their GDN programme manager, with a copy to awards@gdn.int.

1. The grants at a glance

| Track | Who | Prize amounts (2025-2026 editions) | Mentor title |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| ORD | Researchers from developing countries | First prize USD 30,000; second USD 20,000; third USD 10,000 | Scientific and Technical Advisor |
| MIDP | NGOs and CSOs running existing, socially innovative projects | Two first prizes of USD 50,000; one second prize of USD 25,000 | Technical Advisor |
| JSDF | One of the two MIDP first-prize winners, to scale up its project | Up to USD 200,000 | Technical Advisor |
| Connector Grant | One ORD winner, working with the JSDF grantee | Up to USD 20,000 | JSDF mentor involved |

All awards are grants of 12 to 24 months, implemented with technical, research and management support from GDN. The JSDF and Connector Grant usually start about 12 months after the ORD and MIDP grants.

2. Grants below the first prize: scaling down

Projects awarded a prize below the first prize receive less funding than they applied for and must adapt the project to the funds available. This can require substantial work, as the available budget can be several times smaller than planned. The nature of the project cannot change. GDN and the mentor work with the team to scale the project down to a realistic proposal. This is usually done at the start of the grant, and the revised proposal is approved by the mentor.

3. Tranches and what triggers each payment

Grants are paid in at least three tranches, each linked to deliverables; longer grants can have more, with additional progress reports. The number of tranches, their dates and amounts are in Annex II of each grant agreement.

| Tranche | Typical trigger | Notes |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | Signing the grant agreement (countersigned copy) | In 2025 agreements: 40% for ORD, 45% for MIDP |
| Early milestone (no payment) | ORD: revised project proposal approved by the mentor, if required. MIDP: monitoring and evaluation tools, indicators and targets approved by the mentor | JSDF: the first disbursement is linked to an approved M\&E plan agreed with the mentor |
| 2 | Progress report (Annex III), financial report or Funds Utilization Statement (FUS), and documentation of activities (fieldwork, interviews, focus groups, events, tools developed) | In 2025 agreements: 50% for ORD, 45% for MIDP |
| 3 (final) | Final report (Annex III), final financial report, final study report, dissemination and follow-up plan, technical brief of about 500 words | Usually 10%. MIDP also submits a measurement and evaluation report based on its M\&E framework |

Conditions for the second and later tranches:

A financial report or FUS must be submitted with the bank statement and a reconciliation statement, signed and certified by the recipient (for institutions, the head of finance or of the institution).

Later tranches are released only when at least 60% of the previous disbursement has been accounted for, expenditures for the next period are planned, and all required reports have been submitted.

GDN reviews performance and reporting before releasing a tranche. A tranche can be withheld, in whole or in part, if GDN reasonably believes obligations are not being met and has told the recipient why.

Each report is reviewed and approved by GDN’s programme and finance teams before a tranche is paid. For ORD, research outputs are checked for plagiarism before tranches are released.

4. Reporting

Reporting is set out in Annexes II and III of the grant agreement.

Progress reports follow the Annex III template, which is the same for ORD, MIDP and JSDF (see the document “GDAC reporting requirements and progress report template”). Every report must be reviewed and approved by the mentor before it is sent to GDN; plan at least two weeks for this.

Narrative and financial reports are expected every six months. For projects longer than 12 months, a progress report is due every six months even when no tranche is linked to it, without an FUS.

A Funds Utilization Statement (FUS) is required with every report tied to a tranche, and also, in conversation with the programme manager, once at least 80% of the previous tranche has been spent. The Grantee Onboarding Pack also asks for an FUS within 15 days of the end of each quarter; grantees should confirm the expected frequency with their programme manager (see section 7a).

Photos, videos or other audio-visual material should accompany each report.

The final package includes a dissemination and follow-up plan and a technical brief of about 500 words on the findings and policy implications, for GDN publications.

ORD: the Project Operations Manual expects at least one peer-reviewed research article per grant, revised by the mentor and submitted to a journal on the mentor’s advice. The standard reporting annex does not list it, so ORD grantees should check their own grant agreement and confirm with their programme manager.

JSDF: one final project event per grant is expected, and GDN monitors through site visits at the beginning, mid-term and end of the project.

The mentor submits brief reports to GDN at the same stages as the grantee’s deliverables.

5. Monitoring and evaluation

Each grant needs its own M\&E framework, with a baseline that is specific to the project and that also includes the indicators GDAC reports on as a programme.

The programme-level indicators include: individuals in the target population who benefited directly; the share of vulnerable and marginalised people among beneficiaries, as defined for the project; new collaborations that emerged during or after the grant; dissemination events aimed at policy representatives and development partners; manuscripts accepted for publication; and, for ORD, peer-reviewed articles submitted.

MIDP and JSDF grantees are asked to define realistic targets for beneficiaries, broken down by gender and by whether they belong to marginalised or most vulnerable groups.

Mentors help validate the baseline and the quality of the M\&E plan and data collection.

Grantees agree to take part in GDN’s monitoring and evaluation activities, including the Annual Grantee Survey, for up to two calendar years after implementation ends. This obligation continues after the grant agreement ends.

6. Changes to the project

Any change to the project, budget, workplan or team must be justified in detail and approved by GDN and the mentor before it is made. The progress report template asks grantees to say whether changes have been approved and when.

The work plan and budget may be adjusted only by mutual agreement in writing. Changes to the agreement itself (purpose, payments, Annexes, dates) require a formal amendment signed by both parties.

Written approval from GDN is needed before incurring any additional costs or taking on additional tasks.

Unused funds at the end of the project, or on termination, must be refunded within 45 days, including any interest earned. A grantee who wishes to propose another use for unused funds must submit a written proposal at least 30 days before the agreement expires.

Any change in the recipient’s legal status must be reported to GDN immediately.

If something outside the grantee’s control prevents or delays the work (force majeure), the grantee must notify GDN in writing immediately.

7. Financial management, procurement and records

Grant funds may be used only for eligible expenditures under the project and in line with the approved budget.

Goods and services must be procured in line with the World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers (the edition named in the grant agreement) and the project proposal.

Accounts and records must follow generally accepted accounting principles and be kept for seven years after project completion. Supporting documents (bills, invoices, receipts, vouchers) must be kept and made available on request; expenditures without evidence can be disallowed.

Interest earned on grant funds must be used for the project.

GDN may request access to records with 20 working days’ notice, and may arrange an independent audit. The World Bank may also inspect accounts and records.

MIDP and JSDF: grant funds must be held in a separate bank account with separate ledgers, used only for the grant. An annual audited financial report, including a separate schedule of grant funds received and used, must be sent to GDN within six months of the end of the organisation’s financial year. A yearly financial self-assessment is also required, and GDN assesses fiduciary capacity before and during implementation.

ORD: the researcher’s affiliated institution endorses all Funds Utilization Statements.

7a. Preparing the Funds Utilization Statement (FUS)

The FUS is GDN’s template for tracking budget expenditure. In brief, from GDN’s FUS submission guidelines:

Project information: the approved project title, programme category, main contact, institution and statement period. The total grant amount must match the signed grant agreement exactly, and the amount received so far must follow the disbursement plan; neither should be reduced for bank transaction fees. The balance in hand is calculated automatically.

Budget table: list every line item from the approved budget under its heading (for example Personnel, Data Collection, Other). For each, enter the approved budget, the disbursements (spent to date) and the commitments (accrued or planned but not yet paid). Variance columns calculate automatically. If a budget change was approved, enter the new amount and explain it in the notes column (for example “Approved change by GDN from X to Y”).

Any deviation from the approved budget must be proposed to and approved by the programme manager during the project.

Submission: send a draft to the programme manager for approval first, to avoid having to collect signatures twice. Once approved, have it signed; for institutions the Head of Finance or of the institution must sign. The person who verifies the statement must be different from the person who prepared it. Then email the signed FUS to the programme manager, with the bank statement and reconciliation statement required before any tranche is released.

8. Integrity and anti-corruption

The World Bank Anti-Corruption Guidelines apply to every grant. GDN provides a copy before implementation, and staff may be asked to take part in fraud and corruption training organised with the World Bank.

Non-compliance can lead to ineligibility for World Bank-financed contracts, cross-debarment and a request to refund funds.

Funded work must be new and original. Plagiarism, or using work done for another project to meet the grant’s terms, can lead to a demand for repayment.

9. Publications, data and confidentiality

Grantees may publish and use their findings. Before publication, GDN must be given 30 days to review, without interfering with the technical and scholarly content.

All publications and documents related to the project must carry this notice in a visible place: “The project discussed in this publication has been supported by the Global Development Network (GDN) and Ministry of Finance, Government of Japan under the Global Development Awards Competition. The views expressed in this article are not necessarily those of GDN or Ministry of Finance, Government of Japan.”

Data collected under the project, with adequate documentation (survey instruments, code sheet, construction of variables), must be placed in the public domain within twelve months of the end of the study, unless another schedule is agreed.

Materials, data sets and primary data produced under the grant are co-owned by the recipient and GDN.

Grantees must comply with applicable laws, including rules on research with human subjects, privacy and national data protection requirements.

Information marked confidential by either party must not be shared without written consent.

10. Visibility and the grievance mechanism

The visibility of Japan is very important in this Japanese-funded award; the Japan flag is the key logo. Grantees must invite Japanese embassy or JICA officials in their country to grant-related events.

Grantees must not present the grant as an endorsement of their organisation by GDN, nor make commitments on GDN’s behalf.

Project webpages and published documents must state: “If you have a grievance regarding the implementation of this project or towards its future impacts, send an email to awards@gdn.int, with a copy to fobino@gdn.int, or check www.gdn.int/awards/grievance for further instructions.” This is not required for small posts on social media.

Anyone (an individual, community or organisation) can submit a complaint, including anonymously, at no cost and without retribution. GDN acknowledges it, decides within 10 business days whether it proceeds, and then works with the grantee on a solution and timeline within 30 days, acting as mediator. Complaints are handled in English.

11. When things go seriously wrong

If either party breaches a material condition and does not remedy it within a reasonable time, the agreement can be terminated with 14 calendar days’ written notice.

GDN can also terminate with 14 days’ notice if it is not satisfied with the grantee’s ability to implement the project, the quality of outcomes, the rate of progress, or financial management.

In case of material breach, GDN can ask for disbursed funds to be refunded in full or in part.

Disputes are first resolved by informal negotiation, then by a mediator or arbitrator chosen by both parties.

Raising problems early with the mentor and the GDN programme manager is the best way to avoid any of this. The Grantee Onboarding Pack and the Risk and Escalation FAQ and Guide explain who to contact and when.

12. JSDF and Connector Grant

JSDF:

The two MIDP first-prize winners (exceptionally the second prize, if justified and agreed with the World Bank) can compete for the JSDF grant once their MIDP grant has completed one full year of implementation, provided their organisation is based in a low-income or lower-middle-income country, their performance has not raised serious unaddressed concerns, and their MIDP grant has not been terminated for over six months (the wording used in the Project Operations Manual).

Applicants submit an application in GDN’s format with audited financial statements for the last three years and a registration certificate (both mandatory), annual reports if available, and the list and functioning of the governing body. An independent internal control review of the organisation is carried out.

During implementation, the first disbursement is linked to an approved M\&E plan agreed with the mentor, GDN makes site visits at the beginning, mid-term and end, and the grant ends with a final project event.

JSDF progress reports use the same template as MIDP.

Connector Grant:

After the JSDF winner is selected, ORD winners from the same edition are invited to submit a concept note for research that helps measure and analyse the JSDF project’s scale-up. The JSDF grantee and its mentor comment, a full application follows, and GDN decides in consultation with the World Bank.
