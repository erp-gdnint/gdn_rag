The narrative that follows briefly explains each document: what it is, who it is for and when it is likely most useful, with a line on why each set was created. The documents

01 Needs Assessment

Why it was created: GDAC had never had an internal needs assessment. It is the evidence behind everything else in the folder.

Needs Assessment Report (December 2025). Follows the GDAC user journey through seven stages, from pre-application to alumni, using a survey of 99 applicants and grantees, a focus group for each track (ORD, MIDP and JSDF) and interviews with five GDN staff. It ends with three scenarios for the programme and the trade-offs of each.

For: GDN leadership and the programme team. When: planning future editions, setting priorities, explaining the other materials.

M\&E Overview (November 2026). A short overview, built on the needs assessment findings, of how GDN could monitor GDAC and learn from it over time. It organises possible measures around three questions: are individuals learning and getting useful support; is the programme running smoothly, and where does friction persist; and does the value last and spread beyond the grant.

For: GDN leadership and the programme team. When: planning how to track and report on future editions.

De-identified data sources for the needs assessment report. The survey, focus group, and interview data that comprised the primary data collected for the report are available here. They can be utilized as a reference or for potential further research or inputs for programme refinements or future planning.

For: GDN leadership and the programme team. When: planning future editions, setting priorities, further related research.

02 Frappe MEL Micro-Explanations

Why they were created: applicants and grantees often confuse outputs, outcomes and indicators, which weakens proposals and causes problems at reporting. Further grantees need to be able to explain the impact they have created in a credible and compelling way. The application criteria are fixed, so the units explain them rather than change them.

Frappe Course Index. One page with the Frappe course link and a short overview of the units.

For: applicants and grantees When: whenever GDN shares the units

MEL Micro-Explanations. All three units in one document: Outputs and Outcomes; Building Useful Indicators; Using Evidence to Tell an Impact Story. Each has a plain explanation, a worked example, a practice activity, a reflection prompt and further reading, with room for alumni clips.

For: applicants and grantees; mentors as a shared reference When: units 1-2 at proposal stage and onboarding, unit 3 at final reporting

Micro-Explanation Template. A fill-in structure with guidance notes for writing a new unit in the same format.

For: the GDN team When: when a new concept needs a unit

03 Mentorship Suite

Why it was created: mentorship has always been valued but was often implemented informally and with variability. These documents make roles, timing and escalation clear, to prevent the delays that come from common, avoidable problems.

1 Mentorship Strategy. How the new tools build on GDN’s existing mentoring principles and fit together, and how they could later be built into ERPNext.

For: GDN staff and mentors When: at the start of each cycle; read first

2 Mentor Reference Sheet. What the role is and is not, time commitment, deliverables and payments, working with the grantee, and what to do if something goes wrong.

For: mentors When: at onboarding

3 Grantee Onboarding Pack. The programme and its tracks, the four phases of a grant, who does what, the first month with a mentor, MEL basics, reporting requirements and what to do when things get difficult.

For: grantees, with a copy to the mentor When: at award, before the first meeting

4 First Meeting Agenda. Introductions, project status (including rescaling for 2nd and 3rd prizes), drafting the communication plan and next steps.

For: mentor and grantee When: their first meeting

5 Communication Plan Template. Contacts, how the pair will communicate, milestone touchpoints, staying in touch between milestones, what to do if something goes wrong, and sign-off.

For: mentor-grantee pairs, with GDN When: agreed at kickoff, sent to GDN with the month-one report

6 Risk and Escalation FAQ and Guide. Common challenges in six themes, from agreements and finances to the mentor relationship and external risks, each with typical scenarios, what helps and notes from GDN.

For: mentors, grantees and GDN When: whenever something starts to slip, ideally before

7 Mentor Brief Report Template. A report of about two pages, filling in only what applies at each stage.

For: mentors When: kickoff, mid-term and closeout

04 Alumni Engagement

Why it was created: alumni are an underused resource. Their stories make the units practical and help GDN learn from past grants.

Alumni Contribution Guide. Originally developed to invite awardees to record a one to two minute audio or video clip linked to one of the micro explanations, with talking points, preparation tips and simple recording advice. Could be the basis for other similar types of requests to alumni and/or current grantees.

For: current and past awardees When: when GDN invites contributions, ideally before the units are published
